SummaryGENERATED · EDITABLE

⋮⋮

Permit-status confusion is the top driver of 311 calls and counter visits for Permitting Services — 41% of permit-related contacts ask "what does my status mean and what do I do now?" This moderated study with eight residents will reveal how people interpret the current status messages and where the experience fails to tell them what comes next, informing the status-page and notification redesign ahead of the Q1 portal release.

ProblemGENERATED + CANONICAL

⋮⋮

Residents applying for permits check status through the online portal, but the messages they find are written in internal process language. "In Review — Corrections Issued" tells a plans examiner exactly where an application sits; it tells a homeowner nothing about whether they should wait, upload something, or call. The result is measurable: 41% of permit-related 311 contacts are status-interpretation questions[D1], and counter staff report the same questions dominating walk-ins.

Prior touchpoint research suggests the failure is not findability — residents locate their status easily — but interpretation and next-step clarity[D2]. The Q1 portal release is the window to fix the status experience; this study provides the evidence for which messages fail and what residents need instead.

Target barriers for validation

⋮⋮
TB-001Residents cannot accurately interpret what a given permit status message means in terms of their current situation or required action. — OBJ-001: understanding how residents interpret permit status messages
TB-002Status vocabulary is internal process language ("In Review — Corrections Issued") rather than resident-facing language. — Counter-staff intake log, Q2 2026
TB-003Status notifications do not state whether the resident needs to act, so every ambiguous message becomes a call or counter visit. — 311 call-driver analysis

What we’ll learnCANONICAL · EDITABLE

⋮⋮
OBJ-001Understand how residents interpret permit status messages, what information they expect at each stage, and which parts of the current experience make it difficult to know what to do next.
OBJ-002Evaluate whether the current status vocabulary communicates claim state and required action accurately across resident segments.
OBJ-003Document the strategies and workarounds residents use when a status message is ambiguous, including when and why they call or visit the counter.

Research questions

⋮⋮
RQ-001How do residents interpret the permit status messages currently displayed, and do their interpretations align with the intended meaning of each status?Primary
RQ-002What information do residents expect to see at each stage of the permit process, and how does that compare to what is currently provided?Primary
RQ-003Which parts of the permit status experience make it most difficult for residents to determine what action, if any, they need to take next?Primary
RQ-004When residents encounter an unfamiliar or ambiguous permit status message, what strategies do they use to try to understand it?Secondary
RQ-005Are there specific status stages or transitions where residents feel most uncertain or confused about the meaning of the message?Secondary
RQ-006What language, labels, or additional context would residents find most helpful in clarifying what a permit status means and what comes next?Exploratory

MethodGENERATED · EDITABLE

READ-ONLY · SYSTEM

Approach — Moderated usability sessions

⋮⋮

Sessions pair status-message interpretation tasks (residents read real status messages and explain what they mean and what they would do) with a walkthrough of each participant’s own active permit where available. Interpretation tasks answer RQ-001 and RQ-003 directly; the think-aloud walkthrough surfaces the expectation gaps behind RQ-002. Sessions are remote, 60 minutes, recorded with participant consent; data collection combines think-aloud protocol, screen recording, observer notes, and a post-task message-comprehension check.
