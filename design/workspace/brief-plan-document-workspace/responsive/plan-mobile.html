SummaryGENERATED · EDITABLE

⋮⋮

This study will identify where the permit portal’s status messages fail residents — what each message is taken to mean, what information residents expect at each stage, and where the experience leaves them unsure whether to act. Through eight moderated remote sessions across three resident segments, the research will produce evidence-based message and next-step recommendations for the Q1 portal release, with findings delivered ahead of the November 2 planning deadline.

BackgroundGENERATED · EDITABLE

⋮⋮

Status-interpretation questions account for 41% of permit-related 311 contacts, and the approved brief committed this study to three target barriers: residents cannot interpret what a status means for their situation (TB-001), the vocabulary is internal process language (TB-002), and notifications never say whether action is needed (TB-003). The Q1 portal release is the decision window. This plan operationalizes the brief’s commitments into session protocols, recruitment, and analysis, with findings due before November 2.

ObjectivesSYSTEM · READ-ONLY

READ-ONLY · SYSTEM

What we aim to learn — inherited from the approved brief:

OBJ-001Understand how residents interpret permit status messages, what information they expect at each stage, and which parts of the current experience make it difficult to know what to do next.
OBJ-002Evaluate whether the current status vocabulary communicates claim state and required action accurately across resident segments.
OBJ-003Document the strategies and workarounds residents use when a status message is ambiguous, including when and why they call or visit the counter.

Research questionsSYSTEM · READ-ONLY

READ-ONLY · SYSTEM
RQ-001How do residents interpret the permit status messages currently displayed, and do their interpretations align with the intended meaning of each status?Primary
RQ-002What information do residents expect to see at each stage of the permit process, and how does that compare to what is currently provided?Primary
RQ-003Which parts of the permit status experience make it most difficult for residents to determine what action, if any, they need to take next?Primary
RQ-004When residents encounter an unfamiliar or ambiguous permit status message, what strategies do they use to try to understand it?Secondary
RQ-005Are there specific status stages or transitions where residents feel most uncertain or confused about the meaning of the message?Secondary
RQ-006What language, labels, or additional context would residents find most helpful in clarifying what a permit status means and what comes next?Exploratory

MethodGENERATED · EDITABLE

⋮⋮

Approach — Moderated remote sessions combining status-message interpretation tasks with a walkthrough of the participant’s own active permit. Interpretation tasks present the current production message set (snapshotted before fieldwork) and ask what each message means and what the resident would do next — exercising TB-001 and TB-002 directly. The walkthrough surfaces expectation gaps and the moments a message triggers a call or counter visit (TB-003).

Session format — 60-minute remote sessions via video conference with screen share. Participants use their own devices; a staging-portal test application stands in for participants without an active permit.

Data collection — Think-aloud protocol, screen recording, observer notes, post-task message-comprehension check, and a short structured closing interview.

ParticipantsGENERATED + CANONICAL

⋮⋮

Recruit 8 participants across 3 segments to ensure coverage of the approved criteria:

  • 4 residents with an active permit application — sessions review their real status history
  • 2 first-time applicants (past 12 months) — no learned workarounds; closest to counter-reported confusion
  • 2 contractors / frequent filers — test whether experience resolves ambiguity or routes around it
⋮⋮

Recruitment — Permit-applicant contact list (opt-in), 311 follow-up invitations, counter intercept cards; community-board outreach as backup.
